# Unit 6: Master Class: All 5 Units

## Golden Points
1. Plan vs Adapt: traditional is plan driven, agile adapts to change
2. Stub is the called module in top down, driver is the caller in bottom up
3. Cyclomatic V of G = E minus N plus 2 = number of independent paths
4. BVA gives 4n plus 1 cases, robust 6n plus 1, worst case 5 to the power n
5. COCOMO effort = a KLOC to the b times EAF, time = c effort to the d
6. McCall: CRUIE, MFT, PRI. CMM: Initial, Repeatable, Defined, Managed, Optimizing
7. Choose model: clear waterfall, unclear prototype, risk spiral, test V, fast RAD, changing agile
8. Low coupling and high cohesion is good design
9. Verification is building it right, validation is building the right thing
10. Link every topic to the SDLC chain and write definition, diagram, points, example, table, conclusion

### Scene 7: FDD: Model > Feature list > Plan > Design > Build
    ## Domain object modelling in FDD
    Class model of the business domain made with domain experts
    Shared understanding, features hang on the model
    ## Contribution
    Consistent design, less rework, easy progress tracking
    ## Challenges
    Needs domain experts, upfront time
    Large domains make the model complex, hard to keep updated
    ! FDD = feature centred, model first

### Scene 15: Low coupling + High cohesion = good design
    ## Coupling vs Cohesion
    Coupling: interdependence between modules
    Cohesion: strength of relation inside a module
    ## Design principle
    Low coupling: change in one module does not spread
    High cohesion: module does one job well
    ! Good design = low coupling and high cohesion
    ## Impact on quality
    Better maintainability, testability, reusability

### Scene 16: Styles: Layered, Client server, Pipe filter, MVC, Microservices
    ## Architecture styles
    Layered, client server, pipe and filter
    MVC, microservices, event driven, repository
    ## ADR template components
    Title: short name of decision
    Status: proposed, accepted, superseded
    Context: problem and forces
    Decision: what we chose and why
    Consequences: benefits and trade offs
    ! Record every important decision

### Scene 17: Waterfall: phases ek baar, peeche wapas nahi
    ## Waterfall
    Linear sequential, each phase finishes before next
    ## Advantages
    Simple, easy to manage, good documentation
    ## Problems
    Real projects rarely follow it, requirements change
    Working software only at the end
    Customer sees product very late
    Risks and errors found late
    ! Best for small projects with clear fixed requirements

### Scene 18: Prototype > Customer feedback > Refine > repeat
    ## Prototyping
    Working sample built early to clarify requirements
    Types: throwaway and evolutionary
    ## Advantages
    Unclear requirements become clear, high user involvement
    ## Disadvantages
    Poor design if prototype is kept, many iterations, scope creep
    ! Use when requirements are unclear

### Scene 19: Incremental: part by part. Each increment = working software
    ## Incremental model
    Requirements split into increments
    Each increment goes through design, code, test and is delivered
    ## Advantages
    Early working software, lower risk, easier changes
    ## Disadvantages
    Needs good planning and architecture, integration effort
    ! Example: messaging app, then calls, then status

### Scene 20: V model: har phase ka apna test level
    ## V model
    Each development phase is paired with a testing phase
    Requirements with acceptance, design with system
    Architecture with integration, module design with unit
    ## Pros and cons
    Test planning early, but rigid and no early prototype
    ! Left = verification, right = validation

### Scene 21: Spiral = Plan > Risk analysis > Engineer > Evaluate, loops
    ## Spiral model
    Risk driven, combines prototyping and waterfall
    Four quadrants in every loop
    ## Risk handling
    Risk analysis and prototype at every loop
    Stop or change plan if risk too high
    ## Pros / cons
    Best for large risky projects, but costly, needs risk expertise
    ! Spiral = risk handling model

### Scene 22: RAD: 60 to 90 days, reuse. RUP: 4 phases
    ## RAD
    Very short cycle using reusable components and parallel teams
    Phases: business, data, process modelling, application generation, testing
    Needs skilled team, only for modular systems
    ## RUP
    Inception, elaboration, construction, transition
    Use case driven, architecture centric, iterative
    ! RAD = speed, RUP = disciplined iterative

### Scene 23: Problem dekho, model chuno (Omnitrix)
    ## Choosing a model
    Match the model to requirement clarity, risk, time
    ! Fixed and clear: Waterfall. Risky: Spiral. Changing: Agile
    ~ Trick: Clear-W, Unclear-P, Risk-S, Test-V, Fast-RAD

### Scene 24: Error (insaan ki galti) > Fault (code mein bug) > Failure (run pe galat output)
    ## Definitions
    Error: mistake made by a developer
    Fault (bug, defect): incorrect step in the code caused by an error
    Failure: system behaves differently from expected when fault is executed
    ! Error > Fault > Failure
    ~ A fault may never cause a failure if that line never runs

### Scene 25: Verification: product SAHI bana? Validation: SAHI product bana?
    ## Q: V vs V
    ! Verification = are we building the product right
    ! Validation = are we building the right product
    ## Do both use test case design and strategies?
    Validation: YES. Black box and white box test case design, plus unit, integration, system, acceptance strategies
    Verification: NO execution. Uses reviews, inspections, walkthroughs, checklists
    ~ V model: left arm verification, right arm validation

### Scene 26: Unit > Integration > System > Acceptance (Pichu > Pikachu > Raichu > Mega)
    ## Levels
    Unit: smallest part, module by module
    Integration: combine modules, find interface bugs
    System: complete software, functional and non functional
    Acceptance: customer decides to accept or reject
    ! Alpha = at developer site, Beta = at customer site

### Scene 27: Top down = STUBS, Bottom up = DRIVERS, Sandwich = dono
    ## Approaches
    Big bang: all modules together, hard to locate bugs
    Top down: test top first, stubs replace lower modules
    Bottom up: test lowest first, drivers call the modules
    Sandwich: top down + bottom up meet in the middle
    ## PYQ link: scheduling and integration testing
    Module delivery order decides integration order
    Late module means more stubs, delay in testing
    ! Critical path modules must be integrated and tested first

### Scene 28: White box = code ke andar. Statement < Branch < Condition < Path
    ## Coverage types
    Statement: every line runs at least once
    Branch: every decision true and false
    Condition: every atomic condition true and false
    Path: every independent route
    ## Example
    Tests: a=5 (true) and a=-5 (false)
    ! 2 tests give 100% statement and branch coverage
    ~ Static testing = review without running

### Scene 29: Flow graph > V(G) > Independent paths > Test cases
    ## Cyclomatic complexity V(G)
    V(G) = E - N + 2
    V(G) = P + 1
    V(G) = number of regions
    ! V(G) = number of independent paths = minimum tests
    ~ Higher V(G) means more complex, riskier code

### Scene 30: E = 8, N = 7 => V(G) = 3 independent paths
    ## Count
    Nodes N = 7
    Edges E = 8
    V(G) = E - N + 2 = 8 - 7 + 2 = 3
    Predicates: node 2, node 3 so P = 2
    V(G) = P + 1 = 3
    ## Independent paths
    P1: 1-2-7
    P2: 1-2-3-4-6-2-7
    P3: 1-2-3-5-6-2-7
    ## Test cases
    n = 0 covers P1, n = 2 covers P2, n = 1 covers P3
    ! V(G) = 3, three test cases

### Scene 31: Quadratic roots: E = 12, N = 10 => V(G) = 4 => 4 test cases
    ## V(G)
    N = 10, E = 12
    V(G) = 12 - 10 + 2 = 4
    P = 3 (a=0, d<0, d=0) so P + 1 = 4
    ## Independent paths
    P1: 1-2-3-10
    P2: 1-2-4-5-6-10
    P3: 1-2-4-5-7-8-10
    P4: 1-2-4-5-7-9-10
    ## Test cases (a,b,c)
    (0,5,5) Not quadratic
    (1,1,1) d=-3 Imaginary
    (1,2,1) d=0 Equal
    (1,5,1) d=21 Real
    ! 4 test cases

### Scene 32: BVA: min, min+, nom, max-, max.  4n+1, robust 6n+1, worst 5^n
    ## BVA a,b,c in [1,100], nom 50
    (1,50,50) Real  (2,50,50) Real
    (99,50,50) Imag  (100,50,50) Imag
    (50,1,50) Imag  (50,2,50) Imag
    (50,99,50) Imag  (50,100,50) Equal
    (50,50,1) Real  (50,50,2) Real
    (50,50,99) Imag  (50,50,100) Imag
    (50,50,50) Imag (nominal)
    ! 4n + 1 = 13 cases
    ## Robust adds
    0 and 101 for each variable: 6 more = 19 cases
    ~ Outputs: d = b2 - 4ac

### Scene 33: Domain ko classes mein todo, har class se ek test
    ## ECP steps
    1. Find input conditions
    2. Divide into valid and invalid classes
    3. Pick one value from each class
    ! 3 classes = 3 test cases
    ## Types
    Weak normal: one class at a time
    Strong normal: all combinations of valid classes
    Robust: also invalid classes

### Scene 34: Decision table: har column ek rule = ek test case
    ## Code (white box)
    2 if new: d += 15
    3 if not new and loyal: d += 10
    4 if not new and coupon: d += 20
    V(G) = 3 decisions + 1 = 4
    ## Branch coverage (3 tests)
    T1 new=Y: 15
    T2 new=N, loyal=Y, coupon=Y: 30
    T3 all N: 0
    ## Condition coverage adds
    T4 new=Y, coupon=Y: 15 (blocked)
    T5 loyal only: 10
    T6 coupon only: 20
    ! 6 tests cover all rules

### Scene 35: Cause effect > decision table. State table: har state x event ek test
    ## Cause effect graph steps
    1. List causes (inputs) and effects (outputs)
    2. Draw graph with AND, OR, NOT
    3. Convert to decision table
    4. Each column = test case
    ## State table testing
    Rows = states, columns = events, cell = next state
    ! Test every state and event pair

### Scene 36: COCOMO: E = a (KLOC)^b, D = c E^d, People = E / D
    ## Formulas
    E = a x (KLOC)^b   person-months
    D = c x E^d   months
    People = E / D
    Intermediate: E = a x (KLOC)^b x EAF
    ! EAF = product of all cost driver multipliers
    ~ Intermediate a values: 3.2, 3.0, 2.8

### Scene 37: Effort = 2.8 x 50^1.2 x 0.891 = 272.8 person months
    ## Step 1: EAF
    EAF = 0.75 x 1.10 x 1.20 x 0.90
    ! EAF = 0.891
    ## Step 2: nominal effort
    50^1.2 = 109.34
    E nominal = 2.8 x 109.34 = 306.14
    ## Step 3: adjusted effort
    E = 306.14 x 0.891
    ! E = 272.8 person-months
    ## Step 4: time and people (c=2.5, d=0.32)
    D = 2.5 x 272.8^0.32 = 15.0 months
    People = 272.8 / 15.0 = 18.1
    ! About 18 people for 15 months

### Scene 38: FP = UFP x (0.65 + 0.01 x sum Fi)
    ## UFP
    40 + 40 + 24 + 40 + 14
    ! UFP = 158
    ## VAF
    sum Fi = 42 (14 factors, avg 3)
    VAF = 0.65 + 0.01 x 42 = 1.07
    ## FP
    FP = 158 x 1.07
    ! FP = 169

### Scene 39: Critical path = longest path, zero float
    ## Forward pass (ES - EF)
    A 0-3, B 3-7, C 3-5
    D 7-12, E 5-8, F 12-14
    ## Paths
    A-B-D-F = 3+4+5+2 = 14
    A-C-E-F = 3+2+3+2 = 10
    ! Critical path A-B-D-F, project time = 14 days
    Float of C and E = 14 - 10 = 4 days
    ## Link to integration testing
    Delay in a module delays its integration
    Stubs and drivers needed, test time squeezed

### Scene 40: EV: SV = EV - PV, CV = EV - AC, SPI = EV/PV, CPI = EV/AC
    ## Given PV = 100, EV = 80, AC = 90
    SV = 80 - 100 = -20
    CV = 80 - 90 = -10
    SPI = 80 / 100 = 0.8
    CPI = 80 / 90 = 0.89
    ! Behind schedule and over budget
    ## SQA
    Reviews, walkthroughs, inspections, audits, standards

### Scene 41: Known (plan se), Predictable (experience se), Unpredictable
    ## Q: Known vs Predictable
    Known: discovered after careful evaluation of plan, technology, environment
    Predictable: drawn from past project experience
    Unpredictable: cannot be guessed in advance
    ## Q: Components vs Drivers
    Components: performance, cost, support, schedule risk
    Drivers: affect the components, impact levels negligible, marginal, critical, catastrophic
    ! Risk drivers decide how badly a component is hit

### Scene 42: RE = P x L.  Sprints = total points / velocity
    ## Risk exposure
    P = 0.4, loss = 5 lakh
    RE = 0.4 x 5 = 2 lakh
    ! Spend at most about 2 lakh on mitigation
    ## Agile velocity
    Total = 120 points, velocity = 20 per sprint
    ! Sprints = 120 / 20 = 6
    ~ Agile risk: short sprints, review each sprint

### Scene 43: Dilemma: perfect = late + costly, rushed = buggy. Quality != Security
    ## Quality dilemma
    Nobody wants buggy software
    Yet perfect software needs unlimited time and money
    Answer: good enough software, controlled cost
    ## Cost of quality
    Prevention, appraisal, failure (internal + external)
    ## Quality vs security
    ! Not the same: security is one part of quality
    Security = confidentiality, integrity, availability

### Scene 44: McCall: Operation (5) + Revision (3) + Transition (3) = 11
    ## McCall model
    3 perspectives, 11 factors
    Operation: correctness, reliability, usability, integrity, efficiency
    Revision: maintainability, flexibility, testability
    Transition: portability, reusability, interoperability
    ! Mnemonic: CRUIE - MFT - PRI
    ## Relevance
    Each factor has criteria and metrics
    Gives a checklist to measure quality

### Scene 45: CMM: Initial > Repeatable > Defined > Managed > Optimizing
    ## CMM levels
    1 Initial: ad hoc
    2 Repeatable: requirements mgmt, planning, SCM
    3 Defined: organisation wide process, reviews
    4 Managed: quantitative control
    5 Optimizing: defect prevention, innovation
    ! Mnemonic: I Really Do Miss Ordering
    ## ISO 9001
    International standard for quality management systems
    Plan Do Check Act, needs certification audit

### Scene 46: Defect density, failure %, MTBF = MTTF + MTTR
    ## LOC numerical
    12400 LOC = 12.4 KLOC, 310 defects, 36 PM
    Defect density = 310 / 12.4 = 25 per KLOC
    Productivity = 12.4 / 36 = 0.344 KLOC/PM
    ## Failure percentage
    5 failures in 200 runs = 2.5%
    ! Reliability = 97.5%
    ## Reliability
    MTTF 400 h, MTTR 20 h
    MTBF = 420 h
    ! Availability = 400 / 420 = 95.2%
